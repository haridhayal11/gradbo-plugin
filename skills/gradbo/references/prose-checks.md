# Prose checks

Use this when the `humanizer` skill isn't available. The aim is text that reads like the candidate wrote it plainly. It changes how things are said, never what is claimed.

Read the draft once for each item and fix what you find:

1. **Inflated words.** Replace "passionate", "thrilled", "leverage", "spearheaded", "cutting-edge", "dynamic", "robust", "seamless", "delve", "tapestry", "landscape", "pivotal", "fostering" and "showcasing" with plain words, or cut them.
2. **Not-X-but-Y and staged reveals.** "It's not just about code, it's about people" → say the point directly.
3. **Lists of three by reflex.** "innovative, collaborative and impactful": keep only what the evidence supports.
4. **Empty openers and closers.** "I am writing to express my interest", "In today's fast-paced world", "I look forward to the opportunity to contribute". Start with the substance; end when it is said.
5. **Claims without evidence.** Every adjective about the candidate ("strong", "proven", "excellent") needs a fact behind it in the same sentence, or goes.
6. **Dashes and colons as rhythm.** More than one em dash per paragraph: rewrite as sentences.
7. **Sales tone.** "I would be a valuable asset" → what they did and can do.
8. **Mirroring the advert word for word.** Use the advert's terms for skills, not its sentences.
9. **Bold labels and headings** in what should be a paragraph answer: remove.
10. **Length.** Within the employer's limit, and shorter is better when nothing is lost.

Then reread it once as the employer would. If any sentence could be in anyone's application, make it specific or cut it.
